## Q1. Skill / agent ecosystem: is there already a skill or MCP that makes background music according to the video's content?

### Takeaway
No skill or MCP server I found analyses a finished video (frames, cuts, speech) and then produces or picks music matched to it. The closest are (a) HeyGen HyperFrames, which picks/generates BGM from a text mood or the storyboard `music:` field and then does the mix, and (b) general text-to-music MCP servers (ACE-Step based ones are free and local). "Matching" is done by the LLM writing a mood/BPM prompt, not by a model that watches the video. The free paths are local ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT code), HyperFrames' free HeyGen-catalog sign-in path, and Pixabay/YouTube-library stock; the generative fallback in HyperFrames (MusicGen) has non-commercial weights.

This is keyword heuristics, not video analysis [READ] — [bgm.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/media-use/audio/references/bgm.md)
- Lyria knobs available if called directly: `--bpm`, `--brightness`, `--density`, `--scale`, `--negative-prompt`; MusicGen ignores all of them (prompt only). - Level and edit rules: bed volume default 0.12 (about -18 dB) under narration, 0.9 for silent film; for short videos check later 5 s sections of the retrieved track and trim from the stronger entrance, add short fade-in and longer fade-out, make sure the track covers the full cut with no silent tail [READ] — [bgm.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/media-use/audio/references/bgm.md)
- Rule in the skill: with no credential, "no credential is not a green light to silently generate locally"; the agent must run `npx hyperframes auth status` and stop for the user's choice (sign in or continue offline) [READ] — [bgm.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/media-use/audio/references/bgm.md)
- `/music-to-video` is the reverse direction: music track (or one generated from a mood brief) in, beat-synced video out. Its `analyze-beatgrid.py` writes `audiomap.json` (energy phases, onsets, rolls, silences, hard_stops, key_moments, phrases, tempo/grid); cuts are placed on the music's structure. It does not choose music to fit existing video [READ] — [music-to-video SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/music-to-video/SKILL.md)

### Inferences
- The "content-aware" part in every found tool is the LLM (Claude) reading the script/storyboard and writing a mood+BPM+instrument prompt (HyperFrames' `inferBgmPrompt` is the clearest codified version). No found skill extracts audio/visual features from the finished video to drive music choice.
- For a Vietnamese creator avoiding paid services, the realistic free stack is: Claude writes the mood prompt, then (a) local ACE-Step 1.5 (via SongForge-MCP or ACE-Step directly) if a GPU of 6GB+ exists, or (b) retrieve from a licensed free library (Pixabay, YouTube Audio Library), then mix with ffmpeg. HyperFrames' free path depends on HeyGen OAuth credits, whose size and longevity are unverified.
- HyperFrames' automatic fallback (MusicGen) carries CC-BY-NC weights, a monetization risk (see Q2 and Q4).
- Most of the MCP servers are young (created 2026, 0-65 stars), so maturity is low; HyperFrames is the only one with a company behind it.

## Q2. Video-to-music and content-aware open-source models/pipelines (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Real video-conditioned music models exist (VidMuse, AudioX, Video2Music, MTCV2M, GVMGen, etc.) but they are research-grade, produce short clips (10-30 s up to ~5 min symbolic), and the two most usable ones I could check (VidMuse, AudioX) have CC BY-NC 4.0 code, so they are not safe for a monetized channel. The practical path is text-to-music models (ACE-Step 1.5 MIT) driven by a mood description.

### Inferences
- A two-stage pipeline is more practical than end-to-end V2M: (1) VLM/LLM watches keyframes and reads the script and outputs a scene list with mood, energy and timings; (2) ACE-Step makes the track from that prompt with BPM/key set; (3) ffmpeg fits and mixes it. This is the "language-mediated V2M" idea (e.g., Phan-Trung-Thuan/Language-Mediated-V2M repo description, a student project, unverified quality) [SNIPPET] — [repo](https://github.com/Phan-Trung-Thuan/Language-Mediated-V2M)
- ACE-Step's time signature/BPM/key control and music extension/repaint features make it the best-fit generator for matching a target BPM and duration, but nobody in the ecosystem yet exposes scene-level energy curves to it.
- Because VidMuse and AudioX code is CC BY-NC and both derive from NC-weights (MusicGen) or restricted weights (Stable Audio Open community license), treat them as research/demo only for a monetized channel.

## Q3. Content-matching methods that work without a generative model

### Takeaway
Practitioners treat it as three solvable sub-problems: (1) retrieve or tag tracks by mood/energy using text-audio embeddings or mood tags; (2) align cuts/beats with librosa-type beat and onset tracking (and verify, since tempo can be doubled/halved); (3) duck and fit with ffmpeg (`sidechaincompress`, `loudnorm`, `afade`, trim/loop). All are free and local. Source depth here is thinner than Q1-Q2 because ffmpeg.org and librosa.org could not be fetched.

### Cited Findings
Mood/tempo mapping:
- HyperFrames' only codified mapping: industry keyword -> base prompt + BPM (92 finance, 100 crypto, 108 default tech, 115 creative); archetype adjusts (+10 BPM for feature cascade, -8 for demo loop, minor-to-major for problem-agitate-solve); Lyria guidance: 90-110 BPM calm, 110-130 energetic, brightness >= 0.7 for promotional [READ] — [bgm.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/media-use/audio/references/bgm.md)
- HyperFrames `music-to-video`: "fast, high-energy BGM" with strong beat grid drives cuts; calm tracks should be paced by phrase, not beat [READ] — [SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/music-to-video/SKILL.md)

Beat/cut synchronization:
- librosa: `librosa.beat.beat_track` returns tempo and beat frames; `frames_to_time` converts to seconds [SNIPPET, librosa docs via search] — [librosa tutorial](https://librosa.org/doc/main/tutorial.html)
- Existing helper scripts: reel-builder `detect_beats.py` (librosa, strong beat = every 4th, BPM-grid fallback); `mvmake` PyPI CLI (music + video directory -> music video, `--method onset|beat|union`); `cut-fx` (beat-aligned cuts with ffmpeg >= 6.0) [SNIPPET] — [tessl listing](https://tessl.io/registry/gamussa/reels-producer-skill/files/skills/reel-builder/scripts/detect_beats.py), [mvmake](https://pypi.org/project/mvmake), [cut-fx](https://pypi.org/project/cut-fx/)
- Caveat: beat trackers can pick half/double tempo or miss events; check, and use onset detection for soft drums (ComfyUI Beat Analyzer docs) [SNIPPET] — [comfy.icu](https://comfy.icu/node/MarioBeatAnalyzer). HyperFrames' own skill says its analyzer's `bpm`/`beats_sec` are reliable only for genuinely rhythmic music, while energy/onsets/silences are always reliable [READ] — [SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/music-to-video/SKILL.md)
- madmom: not researched (no source read).

Ducking under voiceover:
- ffmpeg `sidechaincompress` takes two inputs; one controls compression of the other [SNIPPET via ffmpeg-user thread and Cinelerra bug thread] — [ffmpeg-user thread](https://lists.ffmpeg.org/archives/list/ffmpeg-user@ffmpeg.org/thread/CJH2SEPGGFUCQ3YS4VXB7CKJS5OZAPCN/). - A working production example, read in source: OpenMontage `_duck` uses `[1:a]sidechaincompress=threshold=0.02:ratio=9:attack=<0.2>:release=<0.5>` plus configurable `duck_level`, and `full_mix` adds `loudnorm=I=-16:LRA=11:TP=-1.5` [READ] — [audio_mixer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/calesthio/OpenMontage/main/tools/audio/audio_mixer.py)

CLAP can embed a text mood and rank audio files by similarity: standard use, but I did not read a source describing this for music retrieval; treat as general knowledge.
- Essentia (MTG) mood classifiers: not verified (LICENSE not found at the tested path; known to be AGPL from general knowledge, unconfirmed here).
- OpenMontage `music_library.py` lets the agent present a user's local royalty-free library at proposal stage [READ] — [music_library.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/calesthio/OpenMontage/main/tools/audio/music_library.py)

Licensing/safety of library music for YouTube:
- A HeyGen help article and competitor blogs say Pixabay/Storyblocks tracks can still get Content ID claims (resolved by dispute with the Pixabay license text); only tracks from YouTube's Audio Library are described as claim-safe, and YPP members can monetize videos using it. All competitor-sourced; Pixabay's own terms not read [SNIPPET] — [HeyGen help](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/8986144-how-to-remove-your-content-id-claim-in-youtube), [routenote blog](https://licensing.routenote.com/blog/?p=8171), [foximusic](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/?p=546)

### Inferences
- A no-model matching workflow: Claude writes a scene-by-scene mood table (mood, energy 1-5, BPM range, key mode: major for upbeat/hopeful, minor for serious) from the script, then either CLAP/tag-search over a licensed local library or ACE-Step generation with that BPM; `librosa` verifies tempo and finds the strong entrance; ffmpeg fits duration with trim + `afade` (+ `aloop`/crossfade if shorter), `sidechaincompress` + `loudnorm` for mix. Mapping rules (major/minor, BPM bands) other than HyperFrames' values are my synthesis, not sourced.
- Ducking in HyperFrames/OpenMontage is a mixing step only; neither uses the voiceover transcript to choose music.

### Gaps
- ffmpeg.org filter documentation, librosa docs, madmom repo and Essentia docs could not be read; exact `sidechaincompress` parameter defaults and the canonical multi-input filtergraph unverified.
- No source found that quantifies tempo-to-mood mapping (BPM vs emotion) beyond HyperFrames' hand-coded values.
- Free-library licenses (Pixabay, YouTube Audio Library, FMA, Jamendo) and their Content ID behavior not verified at primary sources.
- Vietnamese-specific music libraries not researched.

### Inferences
- Answer to the user's question: partly. There are skills that generate/select BGM from a mood description (HyperFrames, OpenMontage, ACE-Step MCPs), and skills that mix it properly, but none that automatically derives the mood from the video content itself; Claude must do that step by reading the script or watching keyframes.
