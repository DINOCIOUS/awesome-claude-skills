## 1. Which open-source / free models can legally be used for monetized YouTube background music, and what do they need to run?

### Takeaway
Four open options have commercial-friendly terms on the evidence I could reach: **DiffRhythm** (Apache 2.0 code and DiT weights, instrumental mode, 8 GB VRAM minimum, VERIFIED-PRIMARY), **HeartMuLa** (Apache 2.0 repo and all related weights per its README, VERIFIED-PRIMARY, but instrumental mode and VRAM not documented), **ACE-Step 1.5** (code MIT VERIFIED-PRIMARY; weights tagged MIT only per an unopened Hugging Face snippet, so CONDITIONAL; lowest hardware bar, under 4 GB VRAM claimed, CPU/Apple supported) and **Stable Audio 3 Small/Medium** (Stability Community License, free commercial use while the creator's/organization's annual revenue is under $1M, license text not read, so CONDITIONAL). **MusicGen/AudioCraft weights (CC-BY-NC 4.0) and YuE2 weights (CC BY-NC 4.0 plus a creator exception) must not be recommended** to a user who wants only unambiguously non-NC terms.

## 3. Content ID and platform risk for AI / "royalty-free" tracks, and mitigations (extension of existing notes)

### Takeaway
Content ID claims on AI music are real and mostly come from two mechanisms: (a) the AI tool vendor or its distributor registering generated tracks in Content ID (so a customer or other customers get matched), and (b) outputs that resemble existing works. Evidence is vendor-heavy and anecdotal. YouTube reportedly added a Studio tool in May 2026 that generates four royalty-free instrumental tracks to replace flagged audio, which is a useful remedy. Mubert's own FAQ forbids registering its music in Content ID; users nonetheless report strikes. For self-hosted open models there is no vendor to register tracks, but you still carry output-similarity risk.

### Cited Findings
- A search summary of several vendor/media pages reports that some AI music tool providers have registered their generated music with Content ID, so creators can get claims from the tool company rather than a label; claims usually mean monetization by the claimant or tracking rather than takedown, though a rejected dispute can escalate to a copyright strike. — [Hellothematic](https://hellothematic.com/ai-music-for-youtube/), [Soundverse](https://www.soundverse.ai/blog/article/how-ai-music-is-reviewed-by-youtubes-content-id-system-0033), [Sophie Cress](https://sophiecress.com/blog/suno-ai-music-on-youtube) (vendor/blog sources; claims not independently verified).
- Reason offered for false positives: generative models may produce melodies or rhythms close to existing ones, and tracks lacking embedded rights markers are said to have higher false-positive risk. — via same search summary; plausible explanation only, not YouTube policy.
- May 2026: YouTube added a tool in YouTube Studio that generates four royalty-free instrumental tracks to replace copyrighted audio in a video. — [Mixmag Asia](https://mixmag.asia/read/youtube-studio-ai-music-generator-fix-copyright-claims-tech) (search summary only; I did not open the article or an official YouTube post).
- Mubert: FAQ states "It is strictly prohibited to register our music under Content ID systems"; CostBench reports user complaints of strikes even after a year of use with attribution and a license; Mubert reportedly did not respond. — [Mubert FAQ (snippet)](https://mubert.com/ja/render/faq), [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/ai-music-generators/mubert/hidden-costs/). Anecdotal.
- Soundraw paid plan is marketed as including Content ID protection for YouTube (vendor claim). — [aitoolsatlas Soundraw](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/soundraw/pricing).
- Training-data legal exposure: Udio reportedly denied Sony's claims while acknowledging use of audio scraped from YouTube; Stability AI/AudioSparx was sued in December 2025 per a search summary; Suno and Udio reportedly faced label pressure for Content ID-style fingerprinting. — [Jaybird roundup 2026-05-08](https://jaybirdcom.com/jaybird-weekly-headline-roundup-may-8-2026/) (already cited in the existing notes), [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-upgrades-ai-song-editor-as-labels-reportedly-push-for-content-id-style-fingerprinting-in-licensing-talks-with-ai-music-platforms).
- Every open-model README checked carries a disclaimer asking users to check originality, disclose AI involvement and get permission before adapting protected styles or materials (ACE-Step, DiffRhythm). — [ACE-Step README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5), [DiffRhythm README](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/DiffRhythm).
- Copyright status of purely AI-generated output depends on local law and is not guaranteed. — [Dubspot comparison (via search summary)](https://blog.dubspot.com/stable-audio-3-vs-suno-2026). Not researched for Vietnam.

### Inferences
- Self-hosted open models remove the "vendor registered it in Content ID" route but not the "output resembles an existing song" route. Practical mitigations consistent with the sources: do not register the tracks in Content ID or with any distributor/aggregator; keep a per-track log (model name and version, weights license URL and commit/date, prompt, seed, settings, date) and a copy of the license text at generation time; generate instrumentals only (no lyrics, no vocals, no "in the style of [artist]" prompts, no melody/reference-audio input); do not use audio-to-audio/cover/reference features with copyrighted audio; check generated tracks before use (e.g., upload a private/unlisted test video to see whether YouTube flags the audio before publishing; or compare to known songs with a recognition app); prefer longer, ambient, low-melody textures over hooky melodies, which are less likely to resemble existing works; keep a fallback of YouTube Audio Library tracks, and use YouTube's claim-dispute and replace-song tools if a claim appears. The "private test upload" and "ambient is lower risk" points are practical inferences, not sourced.
- Stable Audio 3 and ACE-Step marketing around "licensed training data" reduces, but does not eliminate, risk; claims are developer statements and no independent audit was seen.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube source confirming the May 2026 Studio AI-instrumental replacement tool (only a Mixmag Asia summary); no YouTube statement on AI-generated music and Content ID.
- No quantitative data on how often self-hosted open-model output gets claimed; the figures in vendor blogs (e.g., OutlierKit) are unverified.
- Did not find creator-forum threads (Reddit, YouTube Help Community) on claims against ACE-Step / Stable Audio / DiffRhythm output specifically.
