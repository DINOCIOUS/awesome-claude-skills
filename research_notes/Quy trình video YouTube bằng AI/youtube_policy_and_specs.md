## 1. Monetization policy: YouTube Partner Program "inauthentic content" (mass-produced / repetitive / reused content)

### Takeaway
YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in July 2025 and says this was a clarification, not a new rule. A further clarification in July 2026 reportedly names three non-monetizable categories: generic or repetitive (template-made) content, "unsatisfying or off-putting" content, and AI personas discussing sensitive topics such as health and finance. YouTube says using AI is not disqualifying, but faceless, template-driven, near-identical AI channels are the core risk, and enforcement is judged at channel level.

### Cited Findings

It surfaced in search, but I could not open it; the wording below comes from search summaries of it. Confidence on exact wording: low to medium.
- On July 15, 2025 YouTube updated YPP guidance to "better identify mass-produced and repetitious content" and renamed the "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content". Rene Ritchie (YouTube Head of Editorial & Creator Liaison) called it a "minor update" and said "This kind of content has been ineligible for monetization for years, and it's content that viewers often consider spam." — via [AlternativeTo summary](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content), [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/). Confidence: LOW; do not use as fact.

### Inferences
- For a faceless AI-voiced, AI-visual channel, the inauthentic-content risk is evaluated across the channel. Risk factors: same template/format/intro repeated across many videos, near-identical scripts with swapped topics, stock or AI visuals with generic narration, and no distinctive human-authored analysis. Mitigations consistent with YouTube's statements: demonstrable original commentary, unique scripts, varied structure, human editorial judgment, and real data or charts the creator produced.
- A finance/trading channel is exposed to the third category if it uses a synthetic "expert" persona (AI-generated analyst or avatar presented as a licensed advisor). A clearly disclosed avatar presenting the channel owner's own analysis appears lower risk but this is interpretation, not an official statement.
- Production framework gates to include: (a) template-variation check across the last N uploads; (b) a "what did a human add" statement per video (original analysis, own data, own commentary); (c) no fabricated credentials or fake-expert personas; (d) channel-level review before scaling upload volume.

## 2. AI disclosure: "altered or synthetic content" label

### Takeaway
Creators must disclose realistic content that is meaningfully altered or synthetically generated (real person saying/doing something they did not, altered footage of real events/places, realistic scenes that did not happen). Not required for productivity uses (scripts, ideas, captions) or clearly unrealistic/animated content or minor edits. The toggle is in the upload Details flow in YouTube Studio; since May 2026 YouTube also reportedly auto-labels undisclosed significant photorealistic AI content and shows labels more prominently.

### Cited Findings
- Official help page: [Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) (a variant is titled "Disclosing use of GenAI content"). Could not open; summarized via search. Confidence: HIGH-snippet for the core rules.
- Disclosure is required when content is realistic and meaningfully altered or synthetic, for example: making a real person appear to say or do something they did not; altering footage of a real event or place; generating a realistic-looking scene that did not occur (e.g., realistic depiction of a tornado heading toward a real city, a missile fired toward a real city, a real person shown being arrested, face swap, simulated audio of a medical professional's advice). The page says these are not the only cases and covers fully or partially altered content from audio, video or image tools. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) (via search summary), [YouTube blog, March 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content) (via search summary).
- Disclosure not required for: clearly unrealistic or fantastical content (animation, obvious special effects), minor edits (colour adjustment, lighting filters, background blur, beauty filters), and productivity assistance such as generating scripts, content ideas or automatic captions. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-introduces-mandatory-disclosure-for-ai-content/), [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/youtube-ai-synthetic-content-disclosure) (summaries).
- Where: YouTube Studio (computer or mobile) during upload, Details section, "Altered content" question (may need "Show more"); answer Yes if criteria are met. A newer version of the Help page reportedly places it under Attributes as "AI use". The control can be changed later via Content then Details (third-party guide). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [Minimatters guide](https://www.minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/). UI naming differs between sources, so check live Studio.
- Label placement: originally in the expanded description (and on the player front for sensitive topics such as elections, conflicts, public health crises, public officials). Videos made with YouTube's own generative tools are labelled automatically. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content) (summaries).
- 2026 changes (reported late May 2026): labels shown directly beneath long-form videos and as overlays on Shorts; YouTube systems scan uploads for "significant photorealistic AI use" using signals such as C2PA metadata and SynthID and may add the label if the creator did not; creators can contest labels in Studio but labels from YouTube's own AI tools or C2PA metadata cannot be removed; YouTube says labels do not directly affect monetization eligibility or recommendation rankings; animated/unrealistic/lightly edited content keeps disclosure in the description. — [Search Engine Journal via StartupMac summary](https://startupmac.substack.com/p/youtube-will-now-automatically-label), [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-expands-automatic-ai-labeling-system/), [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/111874/youtube-confirms-automatic-ai-detection-is-being-rolled-out-targets-specific-videos/index.html), [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/deepfake-youtube-ai). No primary YouTube post seen; sources vary on whether it targets only "deceptive" content or all significant photorealistic AI. Confidence: MED.
- Consequences of non-disclosure (historic 2024 policy statement as reported): repeated failure to disclose can lead to content removal, suspension from YPP, or other penalties; YouTube may apply a label itself. — [dtnext/IANS](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/lifestyle/technology/youtube-to-crack-down-on-ai-generated-videos-via-labels-removals-748281) (snippet only), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-introduces-mandatory-disclosure-for-ai-content/). Confidence: LOW to MED because I did not see the exact wording.
- Likeness and voice: YouTube's likeness detection (face) is Content ID-style; expanded to YPP creators (2025), politicians/officials/journalists pilot (March 2026), talent/agencies (April 21, 2026) and reportedly all creators 18+ (mid-May 2026, phased by country). Enrolment needs government ID plus selfie video. Audio/voice detection was described as planned "in 2026" but not shipped in the sources found; voice cloning must be handled via the manual privacy complaint process. — [TechCrunch 2026-03-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/youtube-ai-deepfake-detection-politicians-government-officials-journalists/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/10/youtube-deepfake-detection-journalists-politicians), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtubes-ai-face-shield-now-covers-every-creator-18-and-over), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-likeness-detection-expansion-3667840/). Confidence: MED. (Whether voice detection has shipped by October 2026 was not confirmed.)
- Vietnam: Vietnamese tech press (ZNews, Viettimes, VnReview, Hoang Ha Mobile) reports the same global rules: creators must self-disclose significant AI content; automatic labelling from May 2026. No Vietnam-specific disclosure rule found. — [Hoang Ha Mobile](https://hoanghamobile.com/tin-tuc/youtube-se-trien-khai-tinh-nang-phat-hien-ai-tu-dong-va-gan-nhan-cho-cac-video-duoc-tao-bang-ai/), [Viettimes](https://viettimes.vn/youtube-yeu-cau-ghi-nhan-video-su-dung-cong-nghe-ai-post173832.html) (titles/snippets only).

### Inferences
- An AI voiceover narrating a script is arguably not "realistic depiction of a real person saying something they did not" unless the voice imitates a real person; a synthetic voice that sounds human narrating original commentary is a grey area. A conservative gate: disclose (toggle Yes) whenever a realistic synthetic human voice or avatar could be mistaken for a real identifiable person, any photorealistic AI footage of real events/places/people, or fabricated "news" scenes. Typical charts, animations, infographics and clearly stylized AI illustrations should not need the toggle.
- Because YouTube may label automatically anyway, and labels reportedly do not affect monetization or ranking, over-disclosing carries low reported penalty versus under-disclosing risk. This is inference.
- Never use cloned voices or faces of real people (traders, public figures) without consent; this triggers disclosure plus privacy-complaint and likeness risk.

### Gaps
- Official page not opened; current exact toggle label ("Altered content" vs "AI use"), current last-updated date and the exact enforcement ladder for non-disclosure not verified.
- Whether an AI-synthesized narration voice (non-cloned) requires disclosure: no explicit official statement found.
- No Vietnam-specific disclosure rule found.
- Whether audio likeness detection launched before October 2026: unconfirmed.

## 3. Copyright and music (Content ID, royalty-free, AI music, Audio Library, fair use, stock/news footage, AI training-derived output)

### Takeaway
Only music downloaded from YouTube's own Audio Library is guaranteed not to be claimed through Content ID; "royalty-free" music from other sources can still be claimed. AI-generated music is allowed but treated as higher risk if fully automated, and AI output that replicates copyrighted works can be flagged. Fair use is case by case and does not shield wholesale reuse of news/stock footage.

### Cited Findings
- Official page: [YouTube Audio Library help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en) (could not open; summarized). YPP creators can monetize videos with Audio Library music and sound effects; files downloaded from the library will not be claimed via Content ID; YouTube is "not responsible for issues that arise from 'royalty-free' music and sound effects from YouTube channels or other music libraries"; YouTube cannot give legal advice (including issues off-platform). — via search summary of the page. Confidence: HIGH-snippet.
- Attribution depends on track licence: Creative Commons tracks require credit text pasted in the description; the library has an "Attribution not required" filter. Standard-licence tracks are described by a third-party licensing guide as limited to YouTube use (CC-BY tracks usable elsewhere with attribution) — [License.org guide](https://licenseorg.com/guide/music-audio/youtube-audio-library) (third party, verify). Some Audio Library tracks may still receive claims from third parties per a creator guide — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/?p=546) (vendor, LOW).
- AI music: allowed but fully automated "AI music channels" are described as the main inauthentic-content target; Content ID can flag AI outputs that closely replicate copyrighted songs. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/ai-generated-music-youtube-monetization-2026/) (vendor, LOW to MED).
- Training-data litigation: a May 2026 roundup reports Udio denied Sony Music's infringement claims while acknowledging use of audio scraped from YouTube; fair use for AI training has not been ruled on in sources found. Labels reportedly seek Content ID-style fingerprinting from Suno/Udio in licensing talks (that report may be from 2025). — [Jaybird roundup 2026-05-08](https://jaybirdcom.com/jaybird-weekly-headline-roundup-may-8-2026/), [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-upgrades-ai-song-editor-as-labels-reportedly-push-for-content-id-style-fingerprinting-in-licensing-talks-with-ai-music-platforms). Confidence: LOW to MED.
- Content ID in general: system that identifies uploaded music and routes ad revenue to rights holders. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/suno-upgrades-ai-song-editor-as-labels-reportedly-push-for-content-id-style-fingerprinting-in-licensing-talks-with-ai-music-platforms) (general description only).

### Inferences
- Safest compliance gate: use YouTube Audio Library downloads, music licensed in writing with a retained licence receipt covering YouTube monetization, or AI music from a tool whose commercial terms are saved per track (keep prompt, tool/plan, date). Avoid prompting AI tools with "in the style of [named artist/track]" or requesting known melodies.
- News footage and stock: use licensed stock (retain licence), original screen recordings of own charts, or public-domain/government sources with verified terms. Fair use is a legal defense, not a YouTube right, and YouTube does not decide it; commentary clips should be short, transformative and attributed. This is general knowledge, not drawn from a fetched YouTube page.
- Vietnamese market data or broker charts (e.g., TradingView, MT5 screenshots) may carry third-party terms; check their licence before showing.

### Gaps
- Could not open YouTube's Content ID / copyright pages ([Copyright](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466) etc.); no verified official wording on AI-generated music and Content ID, or on fair use.
- No official YouTube statement found on AI-training-derived output ownership or liability.
- Verified Audio Library terms of use for current standard licence not read directly.

## 4. Financial / medical / YMYL-sensitive content (finance and trading channels)

### Takeaway
YouTube's Spam, deceptive practices and scams policy bans "get rich quick" schemes and exaggerated money-making promises, and the July 2026 monetization clarification reportedly targets AI personas giving finance/health/legal advice. No official YouTube financial-advice disclaimer rule was found; disclaimers are best practice and regulators have ruled that disclaimers do not cure misleading overall impressions.

### Cited Findings
- Policy page: [Spam, deceptive practices, & scams policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973) (could not open; wording via search summary). Prohibits content that "gets clicks, views, or traffic off YouTube by promising viewers that they'll make money fast" and scams including "Content offering cash gifts, 'get rich quick' schemes, or pyramid schemes". Examples reported on regional version: "Making exaggerated promises, such as claims that viewers can get rich fast or that a miracle treatment can cure chronic illnesses such as cancer"; "Videos that promise 'You'll make £50,000 tomorrow with this plan!'". Page says its list is not complete. Confidence: HIGH-snippet.
- Misleading metadata: same policy lists "Misleading Metadata or Thumbnails" as prohibited (see section 6). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973).
- July 2026 clarification: AI personas used to discuss sensitive topics such as health care, finance, legal issues and medical advice cannot be monetized (see section 1). — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/), [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules/).
- Advertiser-friendly content guidelines govern ad suitability (limited or no ads); a vendor guide says YouTube has strict policies on misleading claims or guaranteed returns. I did not see official text for a finance-specific ad rule. — [Subscribr guide](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-ad-friendly-content-checklist) (third party, LOW).
- Regulatory context: UK ASA ruled that an ad showing "Invested: $20 Income: $974" implied large quick returns and that a "not investment advice" disclaimer did not fix the overall impression. — [ASA ruling Zimran Ltd A24-1265945](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/zimran-ltd-a24-1265945-zimran-ltd.html) (snippet). This is regulator practice (UK), not YouTube policy.
- Prevalence: a Legalaes study (vendor-linked, automated text scoring) reported 41.8% of sampled YouTube finance videos flagged as misleading, trading tips and technical analysis 40.6%, and 32.8% of creators promoting their own paid products. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-carries-41-8-misleading-finance-videos-the-highest-of-four-platforms/). Methodology criticized; treat as context only. Confidence: LOW.

### Inferences
- Recommended gates for a trading channel: no guaranteed or implied-return claims; no "make $X in a day/week" titles or thumbnails; show risk disclosure (trading carries risk of loss; educational only; not personalized advice) in video audio/on-screen early and in description, while remembering a disclaimer will not rescue an otherwise misleading message; label hypothetical or backtested results as such with period and assumptions; disclose affiliate links, paid courses and sponsored relationships; do not present an AI avatar as a licensed advisor or fabricate credentials; avoid copy-trade/signal-selling language that promises profit. Vietnamese financial advertising and securities/forex rules were not researched here.
- Titles/thumbnails for finance should match what the video shows, since metadata deception and get-rich-quick promises are separate violation routes.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube "financial advice" or "financial claims" policy page found in search; may exist under advertiser-friendly guidelines or misinformation policies, not verified.
- No official recommended-disclaimer wording from YouTube.
- Vietnam-specific legal requirements (forex/CFD promotion, securities advice licensing) not researched.

## 5. Technical specs (resolution, aspect ratio, codec, bitrate, audio loudness, captions, thumbnails, metadata limits, Shorts, end screens/cards, chapters)

### Takeaway
Official upload guidance: MP4, H.264 High Profile, AAC-LC 48 kHz, recommended 8 Mbps for 1080p SDR at 24-30 fps and 12 Mbps at 48-60 fps; thumbnails 1280x720 under 2 MB; Shorts up to 3 minutes (since 15 Oct 2024) in vertical or square; chapters need description timestamps starting at 0:00. YouTube does not publish an official loudness target; around -14 LUFS integrated is a widely cited third-party figure.

### Cited Findings
- Official: [YouTube recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171) — MP4 with moov atom at front (Fast Start) and no edit lists; video H.264, progressive scan, High Profile, 2 consecutive B-frames, closed GOP (GOP length half the frame rate), CABAC, variable bitrate, 4:2:0 chroma; audio AAC-LC (or Opus), stereo or stereo+5.1, 48 kHz; keep recorded frame rate (24, 25, 30, 48, 50, 60 are common), deinterlace first; recommended SDR bitrates: 720p 5 Mbps (7.5 Mbps for 48-60 fps), 1080p 8 Mbps (12 Mbps for 48-60 fps), 2160p 35-45 Mbps (53-68 Mbps for high frame rate); bitrates are recommendations, not limits. The page lists no audio bitrate. — via search summary of the page. Confidence: HIGH-snippet. Third-party guides cite 384 kbps stereo audio; unconfirmed officially.
- Aspect ratios: 16:9 is the standard for long-form; players adapt to other ratios. Shorts: 9:16 vertical recommended (1080x1920). — [Postfa.st](https://postfa.st/sizes/youtube/shorts/2025) (third party). Confidence: MED.
- Shorts length: up to 3 minutes for videos uploaded on or after 15 Oct 2024; Engadget reports square or taller aspect ratios qualify, other sources phrase the shape rule differently ("wider than square" is a reading difference); no minimum length. — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html), [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241004-youtube-short-video-length-3-minutes), [Async](https://async.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be/). Confidence: MED. Verify whether the 3-minute limit has changed in 2026; none seen.
- Thumbnails: official Help Center page [Add video thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431) — 1280x720 (minimum width 640 px), under 2 MB for videos (10 MB for podcasts), 16:9 recommended, JPG/GIF/PNG; vertical videos with 16:9 custom thumbnails may be replaced by an auto-generated 4:5 thumbnail on some surfaces. — via search summary. Confidence: HIGH-snippet.
- Title, description, tags: title 100 characters; description 5,000 characters; tags 500 characters total (about 30 per tag from one site). These come from third-party sites, not an official page I saw. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-character-limits/), [TubeRanker](https://mail.tuberanker.com/blog/youtube-title-description-character-limit). Title truncation in search/suggested is often around 70 characters (third-party). Confidence: MED for limits, LOW for truncation.
- Captions: official [Supported subtitle and closed caption files](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734698?hl=en) lists SubRip (.srt) and SubViewer (.sbv/.sub) with basic support (no style markup, plain UTF-8), plus other formats (MPsub, LRC, etc.). I could not confirm WebVTT from the snippet; a third-party guide says Studio accepts VTT. — via search summary; [BrassTranscripts](https://brasstranscripts.com/blog/youtube-caption-formats-srt-vtt-sbv-decision-guide) (third party). Confidence: HIGH-snippet for SRT/SBV; LOW for VTT. Recommend SRT UTF-8 as the safe default.
- Chapters: timestamps in the description; first at 0:00; at least three; each chapter at least 10 seconds; format MM:SS or H:MM:SS. — [Dickinson Media Center](https://blogs.dickinson.edu/mediacenter/2026/01/12/youtube-adding-video-chapters/), [Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/tutorials/how-to-add-chapters-to-youtube-video) (third party; consistent). Confidence: MED.
- End screens and cards: end screen in the last 5-20 seconds, video at least 25 seconds, up to four elements on 16:9; up to five cards per video; link cards require YPP. — [Sociality.io](https://sociality.io/blog/youtube-cards/), [Nexlev](https://nexlev.digitalpress.blog/youtube-end-screen-tips/) (third party). Confidence: LOW to MED; the official end screen help page was not retrieved.
- Loudness: no official YouTube loudness figure retrieved. Widely cited: YouTube normalizes playback toward about -14 LUFS integrated, turns louder uploads down, generally does not boost quieter ones; true peak about -1 dBTP is a common safety margin. — [Wikipedia: Audio normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_normalization), [Youlean](https://youlean.co/?p=30813), [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-master-for-youtube/) (vendor/community). Confidence: MED as practice, not official policy.

### Inferences
- Production target for a standard AI-assisted explainer: 1920x1080 16:9, constant recorded frame rate (24/25/30 fps for talking/graphic content), H.264 High at about 8-12 Mbps VBR or higher, AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo (192-384 kbps is a typical safe choice but not official), integrated loudness about -14 LUFS with true peak at or below -1 dBTP (voice-forward content often lands around -16 to -14 LUFS); export SRT UTF-8; thumbnail 1280x720 JPG/PNG under 2 MB.
- Shorts: 1080x1920 9:16, within 3 minutes, but keep important content inside UI-safe margins.
- Higher bitrates/resolutions (1440p/4K) may improve YouTube's re-encode quality for fine charts and text; this is common practice, not a statement from sources seen.

## 6. Discovery fundamentals: recommendation signals and honest titles/thumbnails

### Takeaway
YouTube's official recommendations page describes signals including watch history, likes/dislikes, "Not interested", "Don't recommend channel", subscriptions and satisfaction surveys, stating that viewer satisfaction (not only watch time) matters. Misleading metadata or thumbnails are explicitly prohibited as spam/deceptive practices, with an expanded clickbait enforcement piloted in India.

### Cited Findings
- Official: [How YouTube recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en) lists signals such as surveys that ask viewers to rate videos (to understand satisfaction, not just watch time), likes, dislikes, "Not interested", "Don't recommend channel", and channel subscriptions; different features rely on different signals. — via search summary. Confidence: HIGH-snippet.
- Click-through rate and watch time: third-party guides treat title and thumbnail as the "packaging" that wins the click and watch time and post-click behavior as proof; YouTube has said it moved toward valuing "quality" watch time and satisfaction. These are secondary interpretations. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-provides-new-overview-of-how-its-video-recommendation-systems-work/), [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-the-youtube-algorithm-works/) (snippets). Confidence: MED. I did not confirm that the official page itself names click-through rate.
- Misleading metadata: the Spam, deceptive practices & scams policy lists "Misleading Metadata or Thumbnails" (metadata, title, thumbnail or description that tricks users into believing content is something it is not, or promises something the video does not deliver). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973) (via search summary). Confidence: HIGH-snippet.
- Clickbait enforcement: YouTube said it would "increase our enforcement against videos where the title or thumbnail promises viewers something that the video doesn't deliver", starting in India, initially removing violating content without issuing strikes; example: title "The President Resigned!" with no such content; date and scope of any global rollout not seen. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/youtube-crack-down-misleading-clickbait-videos-8556876), [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/youtube-action-against-clickbait-videos_id166048), [SAN](https://san.com/cc/youtube-cracks-down-on-clickbait-with-new-enforcement-measures/). Confidence: MED (press; date not seen).
- The July 2026 "unsatisfying or off-putting" category (emotionally manipulative formulas, shock for the sole purpose of views) is another discovery/monetization risk for sensational packaging. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-unsatisfying-or-off-putting-videos-with-demonetization-3387786/), [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/) (snippets). Confidence: MED.
- Unverified figures (e.g., "80 billion signals daily", "algorithm drives 70% of views") should not be cited. — flagged in search results as unsourced.

### Inferences
- Gate: every title and thumbnail must be defensible against the video's actual content; no fabricated events, no implied results the video does not show; AI-generated thumbnails must not depict realistic fabricated events or real people in invented situations (also disclosure-relevant).
- Optimize for satisfaction proxies (clear delivery of promised content, retention, low "not interested") rather than shock; this aligns with both recommendation description and the monetization policy.

### Gaps
- Official page not opened; cannot confirm whether it names click-through rate or watch time explicitly.
- Date and global status of the clickbait enforcement expansion not confirmed.
- No official statement on how AI labels affect recommendation beyond the press report that YouTube says they do not directly affect it.
