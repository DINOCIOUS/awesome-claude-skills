But the CFO's comment was about gasoline above $4 and lower-income stress, comps still grew 2.6%, Walmart RAISED full-year sales guidance, and the rollbacks were financed by tariff refunds. ## 2. US gasoline demand

### Takeaway
The 2025 EIA figures are accurate but describe a year BEFORE the war, and EIA explicitly explains the decline by fuel efficiency (VMT actually rose). The 8.92M b/d single-week figure is not confirmed by an EIA source I could reach; the EIA 4-week average to Aug 28 was 8.9M b/d, -2% y/y — a modest decline, not a collapse.

### Cited Findings
- EIA: U.S. motor gasoline consumption averaged 8.9M b/d in 2025, 1% below 2024 and 4% below 2019; VMT rose 1.2% (FHWA) while fleet fuel economy improved 1.9%; efficiency gains (new ICE vehicles, hybrids) cited; trend forecast to continue in 2026 and 2027; article published April 10, 2026 — [EIA Today in Energy 67426](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67426); [Hydrocarbon Engineering](https://www.hydrocarbonengineering.com/the-environment/10042026/eia-increasing-fuel-efficiency-leads-to-decreasing-gasoline-consumption/)
- EIA weekly (via fedprimerate.com PDF mirror of the WPSR highlights): four-week average gasoline product supplied -2% y/y to 8.9M b/d for the period including week ending Aug 28, 2026; gasoline inventories -1.2M bbl, 6% below the five-year average — [WPSR highlights Aug 28](https://fedprimerate.com/docs/Crude-Oil/Crude--Oil--Stocks--for-Week--Ending--AUGUST--28--2026---HIGHLIGHTS---.pdf)
- Single-week figure: one summary says demand fell from 8.96M to 8.68M b/d in the week ending Aug 14; the 8.92M figure appears only on [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/gasoline-demand) — UNVERIFIED for "last week of August". Weekly product-supplied numbers are noisy (swings of 0.2-0.3M b/d are routine).
- EIA's own outlook pre-war: gasoline consumption to decline in 2026 "because of increasing fleetwide fuel economy" — [Rigzone on EIA STEO, July 10, 2026](https://www.rigzone.com/news/eia_cuts_2026_2027_usa_gasoline_price_projection-10-jul-2026-184110-article/)
- Retail gasoline: AAA national average about $4.49 in late Sept (about +50% since the war began) — [Good Morning America/AP](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/consumer-sentiment-drops-time-low-prices-weigh-shoppers-136747603); Walmart also had gas above $4 for much of its quarter.

### Inferences
- The draft says the 8.92M figure and the 2025 average show "not a one-off week". That logic fails: the 2025 average predates the shock and EIA attributes it to efficiency. Valid framing: a ~2% y/y decline in the 4-week average is consistent with mild price response plus the efficiency trend; cannot be attributed to diesel.
- The draft's behavioural sentence ("families combining trips, shortening holidays, staying home") is not supported by any source I found; flag as unsourced.

### Gaps
- Could not find EIA weekly series page for the exact 8.92M figure. No EIA/STEO statement found that attributes 2026 demand decline to price rather than efficiency. No diesel (distillate) product-supplied y/y data found; this matters because the thesis is about diesel demand.

## 3. Labor market: JOLTS, jobs report

### Takeaway
Newer data exist than the draft uses. JOLTS for August (released Sept 29) shows openings falling to 7.08M with hires/quits rates flat; the September jobs report (Oct 2) was weak (+29k, unemployment 4.2%), with downward revisions. This supports "soft labor market" but the Fed cited labor-market STRENGTH in its Sept 16 hike, and none of this can be causally tied to diesel.

### Cited Findings
- JOLTS July 2026 (released Sept 1): hires rate "little changed at 3.2%"; quits rate "little changed at 1.9%"; hires about 5.1M, quits about 3.1M; openings about 7.27M — [BLS archive jolts_09012026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_09012026.htm); [investinglive](https://investinglive.com/news/jolts-job-openings-7-271m-vs-7-300m-estimate/)
- JOLTS August 2026 (released Sept 29): openings 7.079M (vs 7.228M est.; openings rate 4.3%; lowest since March; drop concentrated in firms with 1-9 employees, -335k); hires +46k to 5.192M (all from government +47k; private -1k); quits -23k to 3.066M, rate 1.9% (joint lowest since June 2020); layoffs -61k to 1.641M (lowest since March 2025), rate 1.0% — [BLS archive jolts_09292026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_09292026.htm); [TheStreet Pro](https://pro.thestreet.com/market-commentary/job-openings-quits-and-layoffs-all-fall-in-august); [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/us-job-openings-fall-five-month-low-august-layoffs-656.htm)
- Implied: July quits about 3.089M (3.066M + 23k). The draft's "3.06 million" for July is actually the August level. Hires rate for August not retrieved (5.192M implies roughly 3.2%).
- September jobs report (BLS, released Oct 2, 2026): nonfarm payrolls +29,000 (consensus about 84-95k); unemployment rate 4.2% (from 4.1%); July revised to -10,000 (from +21k) and August to +133,000 (from +162k), net -60k; avg hourly earnings +3.0% y/y ($37.81), slowest of 2026; gains health care +17k, leisure/hospitality +10k, manufacturing +9k; one snippet lists transportation and warehousing +7.6k, but another says transportation/warehousing and retail showed "little change" (inconsistent; treat sector detail as UNVERIFIED) — [BLS empsit archive 10/02/2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm); [Yahoo Finance live](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/live/september-jobs-report-live-updates-labor-market-adds-29000-124648123.html); [investinglive](https://investinglive.com/news/us-september-non-farm-payrolls-29k-vs-90k-expected/); [BabyPips](https://www.babypips.com/news/headline-us-jobs-report-september-2026-payrolls-miss-fed-hike-odds); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/us-jobs-report-september-2026)
- Market reaction: yields fell and stocks rose because weak jobs lowered odds of another Fed hike — [BabyPips](https://www.babypips.com/news/headline-us-jobs-report-september-2026-payrolls-miss-fed-hike-odds)
- KPMG JOLTS July 2026 page (draft's source for "3.3% hires for 2+ years") — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/july-2026-jolts-national.html): not retrieved; claim UNVERIFIED. Counter-note: BLS reports July hires at 3.2%, not 3.3%.

### Inferences
- Draft's "hires and quits flat for 2+ years" is plausible for hires (low-hire regime) but the "quits joint lowest since June 2020" language indicates a lower level than most of 2023-25, so "flat" is imprecise.
- The weakness in Aug/Sept payrolls could be partly energy-driven but nothing found attributes it to diesel; revisions to July (negative) predate peak diesel (Sept). Pre-existing explanations: tariffs, AI-related restructuring, low-hire low-fire dynamics (see section 6).
- Draft's Part 4 test "employment does not weaken further" is already tripped at the margin by the September report (+29k, unemployment up); but the draft's other criteria (crack spread, inventories) are outside this note.

## 4. Consumer spending, sentiment, GDP (aggregate demand evidence contradicts a demand collapse)

### Takeaway
Hard spending data through August are strong in nominal and real terms, while sentiment is near record lows. This is the largest contradiction to Part 3's "demand is falling" thesis.

### Cited Findings
- Retail sales (Census advance, released Sept 16): August +1.2% m/m to $773.9B; +6.0% y/y (strongest since March); control group +1.4% (most in nearly two years); gasoline station sales +3.1% (price-driven) — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/16/retail-sales-rebound-strongly-in-august); [Advisor Perspectives commentary](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2026/09/16/broad-rise-us-retail-sales-resilient-consumer); [Haver Analytics](https://www.haver.com/articles/u-s-retail-sales-unexpectedly-increase-in-august). Nominal figures; with CPI +0.4% m/m, real growth still positive.
- BEA Personal Income and Outlays, August (released about Sept 30): nominal PCE +$190.8B (+0.9%); real PCE +0.6% m/m; PCE price index +0.3% m/m, +3.4% y/y; core PCE +3.0% y/y; personal income +0.2%, disposable income +0.3% (so spending outpaced income, implying lower saving) — [BEA](https://bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026); [First Trust](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/EconomicResearch/2026/9/30/personal-income-rose-0.2percent-in-august). One outlet notes the "yardstick changed" for August PCE — [Wichita Liberty](https://www.wichitaliberty.org/?p=41196); methodology change not examined.
- GDP Q2 2026 third estimate: real GDP +2.2% annualized, revised up 0.7pt from second estimate, on consumer spending, investment, government; PCE price index +5.0% annualized, core 3.3% — [BEA](https://bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-third-estimate-industries-corporate-profits-state-gdp-and-state-personal-income-2nd)
- University of Michigan sentiment, September final: 48.1, second-lowest final reading on record (74-year survey); driven by prices, fuel costs, trade tensions; consumer confidence hit an all-time low in May amid rising gas prices — [Good Morning America](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/consumer-sentiment-drops-time-low-prices-weigh-shoppers-136747603); [Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/economy/2026-05-27/consumer-confidence-reaches-all-time-low-amid-rising-gas-prices-um-study-finds)
- Fed Sept 16 SEP: GDP 2026 projected 2.3% (up from 2.2% in June), 2027 2.4% — [Chase/JPMorgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/markets-and-economy/economy/federal-reserve-raises-rates-officials-signal-one-more-hike-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Sentiment (weak) and spending (strong) diverge, a pattern seen in 2022. Sentiment is not evidence of reduced purchases.
- Some of the August strength is price effect (gasoline) and possible pull-forward; the September data are not out (retail sales due mid-Oct). So the "demand destruction" case cannot be confirmed or ruled out beyond August.

### Gaps
- No real retail sales, no breakdown of discretionary vs essentials, no saving-rate figure retrieved. No Conference Board confidence figure.

## 5. Trucking, freight, farm impacts

### Takeaway
Freight COSTS are surging and small carriers are failing, but freight VOLUMES turned positive y/y in August for the first time since January 2023, which is the opposite of demand destruction (so far). Farm distress predates the shock and diesel/fertilizer adds to it.

### Cited Findings
- Cass Freight Index, August 2026: shipments +5.6% m/m (+5% SA), +2.1% y/y (first y/y gain since January 2023), still -7.4% on two-year basis; expenditures +18.7% y/y and +5.8% m/m; truckload rates +11% in August; diesel +46% y/y and +10% m/m (Cass's measure) — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=580757); [Logistics Management](https://www.logisticsmgmt.com/article/august_shipments_and_expenditures_see_annual_gains_reports_cass_freight_index)
- Earlier trend: Cass shipments declined for 30 straight months before turning — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=562614). Freight recession predated the diesel shock.
- Bankruptcies: at least 16 US trucking/delivery/transport companies filed for bankruptcy between late August and Sept 21 (e.g., Globemaster, Xoco Transport, Jett Transport & Materials, CLJ Transporting, Mill Creek Logistics-Illinois, RP Hay Hauling, Truckload LLC, Pacer Transport); 12 freight carriers filed in March; at least 5 Texas trucking firms since war began — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=581320); [Equipment Finance News](https://equipmentfinancenews.com/news/transportation/12-freight-carriers-go-bankrupt-in-march/); [San Antonio Current](https://www.sacurrent.com/news/san-antonio-news/at-least-5-texas-trucking-firms-have-filed-for-bankruptcy-since-start-of-trumps-iran-war/); [Heavy Duty Trucking](https://www.truckinginfo.com/10173212/trucking-company-failures-on-the-rise). Causes cited: record diesel, insurance, maintenance, freight rates not keeping pace (not diesel alone).
- Diesel price path: record $5.85 on Sept 4 (broke 2022 record), $6.05 shortly after; national average $6.53 (record) cited for Sept 22; AAA $6.32 on Oct 6 vs $3.76 on Feb 27 (+68%) — [WDBO/AP](https://www.wdbo.com/news/business/diesel-prices-are/TABD6HKC6Q7RPHFGUU2VKFI7BE/); [247WallSt](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/01/americas-critical-supply-chain-is-breaking/); [CNBC Oct 6](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/diesel-prices-inflation.html). NOTE: draft says diesel broke the 2022 record on Sept 8; AP says Sept 4 (at $5.85). Also the Oct 6 reading ($6.32) is below the $6.53 high, so prices may be off the peak; check AAA/EIA.
- Farm: as of Aug 7 diesel 54% above 2025; fertilizer up more than $100/ton, nitrogen up nearly 50%; Illinois corn losses projected $131/acre (2026) rising to $167 (2027), soybeans $80 to $138; many row-crop farmers selling below cost of production for second/third year; FTC probing fertilizer prices — [farmdoc daily, Aug 2026](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2026/08/fertilizer-and-fuel-prices-higher-heading-into-fall-2026.html); [KWTX](https://www.kwtx.com/2026/07/16/ftc-investigates-fertilizer-prices-diesel-costs-strain-texas-farmers-ahead-harvest/); [Farms.com](https://www.farms.com/ag-industry-news/fertilizer-and-fuel-costs-squeeze-farm-plans-836.aspx)
- Cost pass-through story: CNBC (Sept 17) "How record diesel prices will rip through the U.S. economy. Trucks, rails are only the start" — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/diesel-gas-prices-oil-us-economy.html); Scotiabank warns food prices may rise as diesel shock bleeds through — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12088214/food-prices-diesel-scotiabank)

### Inferences
- August freight volume rise (+2.1% y/y) may reflect restocking/tariff timing; do not interpret as strength or weakness caused by diesel. Cannot attribute bankruptcies causally to diesel alone.
- Food-at-home CPI was flat m/m in August (section 7), so pass-through to food had not yet shown up; Purdue ag economists titled their August CPI note "The Food Pipeline Still Holds".

## 6. Independent economists' views (demand vs cost-push; dissent)

### Takeaway
Mainstream commentary frames this as cost-push/stagflation-style (supply shock hitting lower/middle-income consumers), not yet demand destruction, and the Fed is treating the economy as strong enough to hike. Dissenting views stress that the economy was soft before the war (tariffs, AI-related labor changes) and AI capex is a counterweight.

### Cited Findings
- RBC's Michael Reid: "another squeeze on the consumer", lower- and middle-income hit disproportionately; diesel +68% since war began, more than gasoline; threat to food and goods prices when inflation already persistently high; described as "cost-push" inflation — [CNBC Oct 6](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/diesel-prices-inflation.html)
- Oxford Economics: diesel is the backbone of freight, agriculture, construction; prolonged-war scenario modeling gives global GDP growth 1.4% in 2026, global inflation 7.7%, a contraction in mid-2026 (scenario from March/April, global, not a US forecast; not realized in US data so far) — [Oxford Economics](https://oxfordeconomics.com/resource/global-diesel-crunch-sends-cost-pressures-down-the-supply-chain); [Business Report, Apr 7](https://businessreport.co.za/2026-04-07-prolonged-iran-war-sparks-global-fuel-crisis-and-recession-fears-warns-oxford-economics/)
- Goldman Sachs (Aug 29 report): raised 2027 US diesel crack vs Brent forecast to $63/bbl from $27 (Europe $49 from $19); global refining throughput down nearly 7M b/d y/y; refinery utilization back to seasonal normal only in H2 2027; CNBC Oct 6 headline says prices must stay high enough to maintain demand destruction — [QCIntel](https://www.qcintel.com/article/goldman-sees-diesel-margins-staying-high-into-2027-65999.html); [Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Goldman-Sachs-Sees-Diesel-Refining-Margins-Soaring-to-63-a-Barrel.html); [CNBC Oct 6](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/diesel-oil-refinery-price-capacity-demand.html). Note the "demand destruction" Goldman refers to is largely global (esp. Asia); one source says high prices and reduced availability are curbing world oil consumption by about 1M b/d vs last year.
