## 1. EIA STEO (Sept and Oct 2026): distillate inventories, diesel price, refinery runs, seasonality, heating season. Is the "below 100 mb in Sept, below 5-yr low through 2026 and most of 2027" claim accurate?

### Takeaway
The Sept STEO language matches the draft almost verbatim, but the draft slightly overstates it ("most of 2027" vs EIA's "much of 2027"), and the "<100 mb in September" forecast had NOT materialised in weekly data through the week ended Sept 25 (105.2 mb). The October STEO (retrieved only via search summary) is more benign on price: retail diesel stays above $6 in October, then eases, with a "slight recovery in inventories" helping cracks fall, and about $4.50/gal average in 2027.

## 2. European gas storage: mid-Sept 65-69% vs 84% 5-yr avg? EU target relaxed 90%→80%? Germany ~55%? Qatar force majeure? 21.5 bcm (ACER)? Latest AGSI+ (early Oct)? Power/heating prices and industry impact.

## 3. Thesis test: crack spread <$50, US diesel inventories recovering through October, employment not weakening. Latest readings; any narrowing in late Sept / early Oct?

### Takeaway
None of the three falsifiers is met. The crack has come off its peak (about $108-116 in Aug/early Sept to about $92 on Oct 6, versus Brent), but it is nowhere near $50; inventories were still falling on Sept 25 (105.2 mb); and the Sept jobs report (+29k, unemployment 4.2%) showed further labour weakening. A logical flaw in the draft's test is noted below.

### Inferences
- Scoreboard (as of Oct 7): (1) crack <$50: NOT MET (about $92 vs Brent; -15% to -20% from peak in a month; the narrowing is real but slow; at the Sept 23-Oct 6 pace of about -$6 per 2 weeks to -$16, it would take much more than October to reach $50). (2) US stocks recovering: NOT MET through Sept 25. (3) Employment not weaker: NOT MET (Sept payrolls +29k, unemployment up).
- Logical flaw in the draft test: path 2 of the draft (consumers cut spending, diesel falls on weak demand) implies a narrowing crack with weakening employment. That scenario is thesis-consistent, yet the falsifier requires employment NOT to weaken. A cleaner test: falsified if crack narrows because supply returns (refinery runs up, product imports up, stocks rebuild) with jobs steady; supported if crack narrows with jobs weakening and gasoline/diesel demand falling.
- The Sept jobs report also fits the counter-argument that the labour market was weakening pre-shock (draft cites JOLTS hires about 3.2-3.3% for more than two years); it cannot discriminate causation.

### Gaps

- EIA Oct 2 weekly stocks not found; October 7 release unreported.
- No evidence found on exact US diesel product supplied (demand) trend in Sept.

## 4. Counter-arguments by analysts: (a) simply an oil shock; (b) structural refining shortfall vs temporary disruption; (c) demand destruction vs inflation pass-through; (d) "98% refinery = no price signal in crude". Named analysts/institutions on each side, including JPMorgan note and Lacalle "Great Diesel Crisis".

### Takeaway
The strongest counters: (a) crude is not actually "cheap" (Brent about $103.6 on Sept 30, Dated $113.48 on Sept 9, and EIA just raised its Q4 Brent forecast by $14 to $105); the draft's $93 is WTI; (c) the IEA sees a record 2.5 mb/d fall in global oil demand in 2026, so demand destruction is already underway in crude as well as diesel, meaning the "who uses less diesel" mechanism is not diesel-specific; (d) mechanism (d) is partial at best. Most supportive: Jeff Currie, AAF, and Lacalle, who argue that refining capacity is the binding constraint, plus a Goldman view that diesel demand is inelastic (cracks +$150 if US ban).

### Cited Findings

**(a) "It is simply an oil shock"**
- IEA Sept OMR (Sept 11): world oil supply -5.7 mb/d in 2026 to 100.7 mb/d (1.3 mb/d below prior forecast); more than 10 mb/d of Gulf production shut in; Gulf exports about 13 mb/d in August (nearly half pre-war); recovery slips to 2027 (+8 mb/d rebound in 2027). — CONFIRMED ([World Oil](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2026/9/11/iea-cuts-2026-oil-supply-outlook-as-gulf-recovery-slips-to-2027/), [OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/economics-markets/news/55404447/iea-sees-oil-demand-decline-deepening-as-middle-east-disruptions-persist), [Hydrocarbon Processing](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2026/09/iea-2026-oil-supply-gap-will-widen-on-delayed-return-of-normal-gulf-flows/)). This is a crude-level shock, but the IEA also says refined product and LPG exports from the Gulf are about 3.7 mb/d below February levels, i.e. a products-specific hit.

**(d) "Refinery at 98% means no price signal in crude" mechanism**
- Support: Currie: refiners can shift yields between gasoline and diesel but flexibility has limits; AAF cites 98% utilisation as a driver; Goldman: US refiners would have to cut runs if exports were banned. — see above.
- Challenges (my analysis from sourced facts): (i) crude is a global price; US refiners at 98% do not set Brent; the WTI discount to Brent ($12.4 on Sept 30) fits a US-specific constraint but refiners in Asia/Europe/Middle East also buy crude, and global runs fell 5-7.5 mb/d y/y, meaning global crude demand from refiners is DOWN, which is the more direct reason crude lags. (ii) Crude was also depressed by demand destruction (IEA -2.5 mb/d) and by stock releases ("crude stockpiles surge on reserve releases" per [World Ports/EIA summary](https://www.worldports.org/?p=191221)). (iii) The utilisation figure has fallen to 92.5% (maintenance), so a "no room" explanation applies less now; the real limit is that there is little spare capacity globally, and runs are constrained by damaged or shut capacity in Russia, the Gulf and elsewhere. (iv) Crude is not below its peak by as much as the draft implies (Q4 point above).
- The mechanism is sound as a partial-equilibrium point: when product margins are huge and refinery capacity is the constraint, the extra value accrues as refining rent (crack) and not as higher crude price. But it does not explain a crack of 5x normal by itself; the spread also reflects product-specific losses (about 3.7 mb/d Gulf product exports) and Russia's export ban. No named analyst was found who states "98% utilisation means no price signal in crude" explicitly.

So "diesel, not oil" is better phrased as "refined products are where the disruption concentrates; both matter".

If Hormuz normalises, both IEA and EIA see easing; if it doesn't, the structural thinness bites.

## 6. Evidence list: what would FALSIFY vs SUPPORT the thesis ("diesel, not crude, is the binding constraint")

### Takeaway
As of Oct 7, readings favour the thesis in the narrow sense (cracks at about $92, inventories near record-low territory, refinery capacity constrained) but the strongest counter-evidence is that crude is rising too (Brent about $101-104, EIA Q4 $105) and that the macro damage predates the shock.

### Cited Findings
**Would SUPPORT**
- Crack stays above $75-80 through October/November while Brent is flat or falling (as of Oct 6, $92 vs Brent). Source: see Q3.
- US distillate stocks print below 100 mb in October (EIA expects below 100 mb in Sept, below the 5-year low through much of 2027; 105.2 mb on Sept 25). Source: Q1.
- Refinery utilisation returns above 95% after maintenance without stock building (97.8% Sept 4; 92.5% Sept 25). Source: Q1.
- A US export ban is enacted and European cracks jump (Goldman: +$150/b). Source: Q5.
- Jobs and gasoline/diesel demand weaken while the crack narrows via demand (the draft's path 2); Sept payrolls +29k, unemployment 4.2%. Source: Q3.
- Europe: storage ends October near 76% or lower, Germany misses its 71% Nov 1 mandate (65% projected), WoodMac's 21% end-of-winter storage materialises, European gasoil premium jumps in Dec-Mar. Source: Q2.
- IEA continues to show product exports from the Gulf about 3.7 mb/d below Feb and global refinery runs 5-7.5 mb/d below year earlier into Q4. Source: Q4.

**Would FALSIFY (or weaken)**
- Crack falls toward $50 with Brent steady or rising (i.e. diesel merely re-converging to crude). Source: Q3 logic. Note Goldman already cites a $53 European crack.
- Diesel retail price and Brent converge in % terms: crude +40% YoY vs diesel +77% (draft); EIA expects retail diesel about $4.50 in 2027 and "easing" from $6+ in October. Source: Q1.
- Hormuz reopening and Gulf product exports restored: crude and diesel fall together (suggests an oil-shock-driven phenomenon). Source: Q5.
- IEA's -2.5 mb/d demand contraction shows crude demand is breaking as well as diesel demand, so the "gap closes by diesel demand destruction" is not distinctive. Source: Q4.
- Labour weakness predates the shock (JOLTS hires about 3.2-3.3% for 2+ years; Renovo and Loparex pre-date it) so employment cannot attribute damage to diesel. Source: draft's own citations; not re-verified.
- Brent (not WTI) shows crude near March highs: Dated Brent $113.48 (Sept 9) vs about $119 peak. Source: Q4.
- Retail diesel falls well below $6 with stocks stable even under high crude (EIA path). Source: Q1.

### Inferences
- The thesis is better framed as a ranking claim ("the refined-product leg has a larger excess than the crude leg") than as an exclusive claim. The best single metric is the share of the diesel price increase attributable to crack vs crude: using $4.60/gal ULSD, crack $92 over Brent about $101, the crack is roughly 48% of the diesel price vs a pre-war about 15-20% share ($20-30 crack on about $70 crude).
