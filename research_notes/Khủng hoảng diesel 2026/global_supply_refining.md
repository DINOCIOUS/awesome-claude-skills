Method caveat for the report writer: WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy on almost every domain (CNBC, CNN, IEA, EIA, S&P, Al Jazeera, Moscow Times, US News, etc.). Several hits are aggregators (roic.ai, hellenicshippingnews, oilprice mirrors, ground.news, straits.live, jingtea blog); I treat them as pointers and mark confidence. Research window: Jan 1 to Oct 7, 2026.

Verdict key: CONFIRMED / CONTRADICTED / UNVERIFIED (plus PARTLY where a claim is right in substance but wrong in detail).

## 1. Iran war timeline, Hormuz status, oil share, March Brent move

### Takeaway
The war began Feb 28, 2026 with US-Israeli strikes; Hormuz has been effectively closed to normal commercial traffic for about seven months and was still so on Oct 4-6 (4 transits on Oct 4 vs ~85/day pre-war). "Partially closed" understates it. Brent's March gain was a record on data back to 1988 (59-64% depending on measure and source), not "the largest ever" in an absolute sense. Brent is currently about $101-105, not $93.

### Cited Findings
- War launched Feb 28, 2026 with US and Israeli strikes on Iran; Iran's effective closure of Hormuz followed — CONFIRMED by [CNBC, Mar 31/Apr 1 coverage via search](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/oil-price-today-wti-brent-trump-energy-sites-water-war-escalation-deal.html) and [CNBC Apr 1](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/01/front-month-brent-oil-futures-extend-gains-after-record-monthly-rise-in-march.html) (search snippets; pages not read).
- Hormuz pre-war flow: 20 million b/d in 2024, equal to about 20% of global petroleum liquids consumption; first half 2025 about 20.9 mb/d, about 27% of seaborne oil trade — [EIA Today in Energy](https://eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=65504) (snippet). So the draft's "more than 20%" is slightly high on the consumption basis; "about a fifth" is the safe wording. The draft's cited source for this is Wikipedia ([2026 Iran war fuel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis)); use EIA instead.
- Status as of Oct 4-6: strait "effectively closed to commercial shipping as of 6 October 2026, with only 4 ships transiting on October 4 versus ~85/day normal"; Iraq's state tanker company moved 2 million barrels on a VLCC through the strait, reported by Gulf News on Oct 5 as the first such operation in decades; Iran's Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf says the strait stays closed until the US meets seven conditions based on the June memorandum of understanding; Trump says renewed strikes after the midterms are "possible" as ceasefire talks stall; war-risk insurance about 30x pre-crisis, 6 P&I clubs withdrew cover — [straits.live](https://straits.live/) and [globalsecurity.org day-220 update, Oct 5](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/ops/iran-war-oprep.htm) (search-snippet summaries; aggregator/tracker sites, medium confidence).
- IMF PortWatch recorded 1 transit on Sept 27 vs a pre-crisis baseline of 85/day — [straits.live brief Oct 2](https://straits.live/briefs/2026-10-02) (snippet; medium confidence).
- Conflicting signal: one aggregator claims US-coordinated shuttling of tankers lifted the 7-day average of oil leaving the strait to "over 8 million b/d"; another report says 40 vessels carrying 18 million barrels transited under US escort (CNN assessment), while the US Navy told the shipping industry that routine escorts are "not possible for now" because attack risk is too high — [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/security/naval/naval-ships/us-navy-tells-shipping-industry-hormuz-escorts-not-possible-for-now); [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/us-navy-tells-shipping-industry-hormuz-escorts-not-possible-for-now/); [Hellenic Shipping News, tanker count](https://hellenicshippingnews.com/tanker-count-on-both-sides-of-hormuz-exceeds-700-as-strait-tensions-reignite). Dates of the escort convoys are not clear from snippets. Treat "8 mb/d" as UNVERIFIED and likely a peak or one-off week, not the Oct picture.
- Ceasefire history: US-Iran peace talks in Geneva abruptly postponed around June 19 ([CNBC Jun 19](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/19/oil-prices-wti-brent-crude-us-iran-deal-strait-hormuz-shipping-recovery.html)); headline "Trump threatens Oman, Hormuz Strait traffic slows, Iran ceasefire ends" on Aug 17 ([CNBC Aug 17](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz.html)). Snippets only; exact ceasefire dates not confirmed.
- Brent March move: front-month Brent recorded a 64% monthly gain, a record in LSEG data back to June 1988 — [CNBC Apr 1](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/01/front-month-brent-oil-futures-extend-gains-after-record-monthly-rise-in-march.html); CNBC headline on Mar 31: "Brent oil price surges more than 60% in March, biggest monthly gain dating back to 1988" ([CNBC Mar 31](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/oil-price-today-wti-brent-trump-energy-sites-water-war-escalation-deal.html)). Other measures: about 59% (Reuters-style wire quoted in [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/global-economy/news/brent-heads-record-monthly-jump-4139601) and others), WTI about 58%. Prior record: 46% in September 1990.
- Brent March intraday peak about $119.50 — [Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2532499) (snippet); Brent May futures traded near $119 in late March. The draft's "above $119" is CONFIRMED.
- The draft's cited World Bank blog ([blogs.worldbank.org](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/strait-of-hormuz-disruption-sends-oil-prices-surging)) did not appear in search and could not be fetched. UNVERIFIED that the blog contains the "65%" and "largest ever" wording. Recommend citing CNBC/LSEG ("64%, largest monthly gain since LSEG data begin in 1988") instead.
- Current Brent: Sept 12 settle $104.61; Sept 2 settle $95.63; Sept 3 $95.52 ([psuconnect, Sept 12](https://www.psuconnect.in/market/crude-oil-price-today-12-september-2026-brent-holds-near-104-wti-at-100) and a Reuters-style wire in search results); Brent about $102.50-102.60 on Oct 5, up about 5.6% on the month ([straits.live](https://straits.live/); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil/forecast), snippet "Brent Crude Near $96" at an unknown earlier date). One snippet also gave WTI about $100 on Sept 12, which seems high relative to a $93 WTI claim. The draft's "about $93 at the end of September", sourced to the Trading Economics generic crude page, therefore conflicts with Brent data found; the draft may be quoting WTI, and I could not confirm the Sept 30 settle. Needs a primary-source check (ICE/EIA daily spot) before publication; the article's "crude still about 20% below March peak" and "$28 cheaper than 2022 record" framing depends on this.
- Pre-war baseline for diesel/gasoil: IEA Sept OMR (via search summary) says US diesel and gasoil prices exceeded $200/bbl in early September, 94% above pre-war — [Hellenic Shipping News on IEA](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1147760) (snippet).

### Inferences
- The draft's thesis (crude below March peak, diesel at record) is weaker if Brent is actually about $101-105 (only about 12-15% below the $119.50 intraday peak). Brent sits above its level of Sept 2 ($95.6) and has risen about 5.6% in the first days of October. A writer should either fix the benchmark or soften "nearly 20% below".
- "Largest monthly gain in the history of the index" is a mild overstatement; "largest since records begin in 1988" is accurate.

### Gaps
- No primary-source Brent settle for Sept 30 or Oct 6-7 found; no Hormuz volume data (b/d) from IEA/Kpler/Vortexa for late Sept/Oct; World Bank blog unread.

## 2. Saudi East-West pipeline

### Takeaway
Confirmed: Saudi Arabia halted the East-West pipeline on Sept 11 (precautionary shutdown) after drone attacks launched from Iraq (Maysan area per Long War Journal); crude flows restarted in the week of Sept 22 but only partially; Yanbu tanker exports had not resumed as of Sept 24 and were still lagging on Sept 28. Full recovery was expected to take six weeks or more. No Oct 1-7 update found.

### Cited Findings
- Saudi Energy Ministry announced a "precautionary" shutdown on Friday Sept 11 after drones targeted the pipeline in the Riyadh and Medina regions the previous day; the pipeline carries crude from eastern fields to Yanbu on the Red Sea, bypassing Hormuz — [Euronews, Sept 16](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/16/no-word-from-riyadh-on-oil-delivery-cancellations-as-scale-of-disruption-remains-unclear); [Al Jazeera, Sept 12](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/12/saudi-arabia-shuts-critical-oil-pipeline-after-drone-attack-what-happened); [Anadolu explainer](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/explainer-what-is-saudi-arabia-s-east-west-pipeline-and-what-its-precautionary-shutdown-means/4054993); [Long War Journal](https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2026/09/saudi-arabia-closes-its-east-west-pipeline-after-attacks-from-iraq.php). Date nuance: one aggregator says the shutdown was on Sept 13; most sources say Sept 11-12. Sept 11 is supported by Argus citing Vortexa: "No Saudi crude has departed Yanbu since September 11".
- Attack origin: drones launched from Iraq (Maysan governorate, bordering Iran), per Saudi Foreign Ministry; Saudi Arabia, at the request of the Iraqi prime minister, held back from responding — [Africanews, Sept 12](https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/12/saudi-arabia-condemns-attack-on-pipeline-launched-from-iraq/); [Long War Journal](https://www.longwarjournal.org/archives/2026/09/saudi-arabia-closes-its-east-west-pipeline-after-attacks-from-iraq.php). Three pumping stations reportedly damaged (per aggregator summary).
- Capacity: normal about 4 mb/d per one summary, "up to five million b/d" per SCMP/Al Jazeera summaries — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3367268/saudi-pipeline-bypasses-hormuz-shut-after-attacks). The draft calls it "the only backup pipeline"; that characterization was not verified in the sources read.
- Restart: Al-Monitor/Reuters reported the restart in the week of Sept 22 — [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/09/saudi-arabia-restarts-east-west-oil-pipeline-sources-say); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/oil-gas/saudi-arabia-resumes-oil-exports-via-east-west-pipeline-after-repairs-1368286); [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/report-saudi-aramco-achieves-partial-restart-on-strategic-red-sea-pipeline). The draft's exact date "Sept 22" matches the Quartz URL it cites (qz.com .../092226); I could not open it.
- Status after restart: crude is reaching Red Sea refineries but tanker loadings at Yanbu not yet resumed as of Sept 24; restart "partial, full recovery elusive" on Sept 28; full restoration could take six weeks or more; interim target about 4 mb/d — [roic.ai Sept 24](https://www.roic.ai/news/saudi-east-west-pipeline-restarts-at-low-rate-but-yanbu-tanker-loadings-yet-to-resume-09-24-2026); [roic.ai Sept 28](https://www.roic.ai/news/saudi-arabia-restarts-east-west-pipeline-after-drone-damage-but-yanbu-exports-lag-09-28-2026) (aggregator, medium confidence). Brent fell more than $2 on the restart news.
- Argus (via Euronews): at least three European refiners had cargoes scheduled for late September cancelled or postponed, in some cases to November; Saudi has not commented; Poland's Orlen securing North Sea, WTI Midland and CPC Blend replacements — [Euronews, Sept 16](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/16/no-word-from-riyadh-on-oil-delivery-cancellations-as-scale-of-disruption-remains-unclear); [Argus original](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2878080-aramco-defers-cancels-some-european-sep-crude-sources). Important nuance: these are Saudi CRUDE cargoes, not diesel cargoes. The draft's sentence is acceptable but a reader may infer product cargoes.
- US Energy Secretary said the pipeline could be back "within days" ([oilprice headline](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Saudi-Aramco-Cancels-European-Crude-Cargoes-as-Key-Pipeline-Stays-Shut.html)); outcome: restart came about 10 days after the shutdown.

### Inferences
- Draft statement "restarted on Sept 22, though capacity still well below normal" is consistent with sources, but by Oct 7 it may have recovered further; I found no Oct 1-7 report.

### Gaps
- No post-Sept 28 update on pipeline throughput or Yanbu loadings; "only backup pipeline" not verified (the UAE's Habshan-Fujairah line also bypasses Hormuz in normal times, but I did not research its status).

## 3. Europe: refining capacity, Korea cargo, retail diesel price

### Takeaway
The Korea-to-Europe cargo is real but is a single rare fixture, not a trend. The "more than 20% since 2009" and "35 closures in 15 years" figures were not reproduced; sources I found give about 17.7% (17.5 to 14.4 mb/d) and roughly 28-30 closures. The $10.70-10.90/gal European price is unverified; EU average pump diesel was EUR 2.14/l in the week of Sept 21.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg (Sept 3): a cargo of about 90,000 tonnes of South Korean diesel loading on the new tanker Priamos for northwest Europe (UK or Rotterdam), arranged by Trafigura, a trip of more than 12,000 miles (19,000 km) likely through Suez; the trade is "rare"; an Asia-Europe diesel swap spread widened to the largest gap in data back to 2023 (PVM) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/europe-s-diesel-crunch-spurs-rare-12-000-mile-journey-from-korea) (read via [EnergyConnects mirror](https://www.energyconnects.com/news/oil/2026/september/europe-s-diesel-crunch-spurs-rare-12-000-mile-journey-from-korea/) snippet). CONFIRMED. The draft's "Europe now has to buy diesel from as far away as South Korea" slightly generalizes one cargo.
- European refinery capacity: since 2009 about 30 refineries closed across Europe and neighbours, capacity fell from about 17.5 to 14.4 mb/d (about 17.7%); another source: 28 of close to 100 refineries closed or converted since 2009 — [European Business Magazine](https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/europe-refinery-closures-diesel/) and search-result summaries (snippet; mixed sources). Another view: Europe lost 1.1 mb/d since Covid and about 400 kb/d since early 2024, five refineries closed in two years — [Argus, European diesel theme page](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/topical-market-themes/european-diesel) (snippet; date unclear, possibly 2025). The draft's ZeroHedge and TodoAlicante citations could not be opened; "more than 20%" and "about 35 closures in 15 years" are UNVERIFIED, and the closest figures are lower. Recommend wording like "roughly 18% of capacity, about 30 refineries" or finding a Fuels Europe/Concawe source ([Fuels Europe PDF](https://fuelseurope.eu/uploads/files/modules/documents/file/1751960869_lzRc52p3Kar5EeCvMqn0QiyD2AjhAltKEUSmhWIS.pdf) appeared in results; not read).
- European supply context: Europe has struggled to replace about 800 kb/d of Russian diesel/gasoil lost after the product import ban (Argus/EnergyIntel snippet, date unclear). Kpler: European refinery runs forecast to average 12.2 mb/d in 2026, +130 kb/d y/y, while diesel demand contracts about 190 kb/d — [Kpler blog](https://www.kpler.com/blog/europes-refinery-runs-strengthen-in-2026-but-demand-struggles-under-elevated-fuel-prices) (snippet; publication date unclear, may predate the war).
- European pump price: EU-27 average diesel EUR 2.14/litre in the reference week of Sept 21, 2026; most expensive Netherlands EUR 2.58/l, cheapest Malta EUR 1.21/l — [fuel-prices.eu weekly bulletin](https://www.fuel-prices.eu/news/weekly/europe-fuel-prices-week-2026-09-21/). Conversion: EUR 2.14/l = EUR 8.10/gal; EUR 2.58/l = EUR 9.77/gal; converting to dollars needs the 2026 EUR/USD rate, which I did not find. So $10.70-10.90/gal is plausible only for the most expensive markets (Netherlands and similar) and the draft's "many European markets" on Sept 11 is UNVERIFIED. One search-engine summary stated "$10.22/gal" for the EU average, but its conversion was inconsistent with its own inputs, so I do not rely on it.
- Europe pays more: Borsa Italiana/Teleborsa headline, Sept 23: "Diesel, Europe pays EUR 203 million more per day" ([Teleborsa](https://www.borsaitaliana.it/borsa/notizie/teleborsa/economia/diesel-te-l-europa-paga-203-milioni-di-euro-in-piu-al-giorno-139_2026-09-23_TLB.html?lang=it); snippet only).
- Goldman Sachs: a US export ban could lower US prices but raise them in Europe; one report says Goldman estimates US export ban could propel (European) diesel cracks by $150/b — [S&P Global, Sept 28](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/092826-us-diesel-export-ban-would-cut-us-prices-but-raise-them-in-europe-goldman-sachs); [QCIntel](https://qcintel.com/article/us-export-ban-could-propel-diesel-cracks-by-150-b-goldman-9256.html) (snippets).

### Inferences
- A defensible line: "Europe has lost roughly a sixth to a fifth of its refining capacity since 2009" with citation to Fuels Europe/Concawe, pending a verified source.

### Gaps
- No verified source for ">20% since 2009" or "about 35 plants in 15 years"; no verified source for Sept 11 price of $10.70-10.90/gal; EUR/USD rate not found.

## 4. Russia: refinery damage and export bans

### Takeaway
Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries are confirmed and very frequent in 2026. The draft's "non-Russian producers" is a mistranslation/garble: the measure applies to non-producers (traders/resellers who are not the refiners), and diesel exports by producers were allowed again from Sept 1. The gasoline ban (all exporters) runs to Jan 31, 2027. Some later snippets mention producer-ban extensions to Sept 30/Oct 31; I could not date them, so the current producer-diesel status after Sept 1 needs a primary-source check.

### Cited Findings
- Government announcement of July 30, 2026: ban on gasoline exports extended to Jan 31, 2027; diesel, marine fuel and gas oils covered; "From September 1, producers' exports will be exempt from the restrictions on diesel, marine fuel and gas oils, while some fuel exports will still take place under intergovernmental agreements and humanitarian aid" — [Moscow Times, Jul 30/Aug 6](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/06/russia-extends-gasoline-and-diesel-export-ban-through-january-2027); [Xinhua, Jul 31](https://english.news.cn/20260731/516988fd069b4d8987ffc9d96d6c4046/c.html); [The Star, Jul 31](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/07/31/russia-extends-full-gasoline-export-ban-until-jan-31); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/russia-extends-ban-on-gasoline-exports/4014065) (search summaries; pages not read).
- Wording that produced the draft's phrase: "Russia has banned exports of diesel by non-producers and motor gasoline until January 31, 2027, and jet fuel until the end of November 2026" and "Beginning September 1, Russian producers will again be allowed to export diesel, marine fuel, and gas oils, but other exporters will remain locked out" — [oilprice.com](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Russia-Extends-Diesel-and-Gasoline-Export-Bans-Into-2027.html) and the Trans.info article the draft cites ([trans.info](https://trans.info/en/europe-turns-to-diesel-500508)), seen via search snippets. The draft's "diesel exports from non-Russian producers" (Vietnamese "các nhà sản xuất không thuộc Nga") is therefore CONTRADICTED. Correct: "diesel exports by non-producers (traders and resellers) and all motor gasoline exports". Also note that producers were allowed back in on Sept 1, which weakens the idea of a complete Russian diesel export ban.
- Conflicting/undated: [Hydrocarbon Processing, Aug 2026: "Russia extends ban on diesel exports until September 30"](https://www.hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2026/08/russia-extends-ban-on-diesel-exports-until-september-30/) and [Mezha: "Russia extends producers' diesel, marine fuel and gasoil export ban through October 31, 2026"](https://mezha.net/eng/news/a8054589_russia_extends_producers/). These suggest the producer-level diesel ban was extended in steps, and the Sept 1 exemption may have been later reversed or these items refer to an earlier cycle (Russia ran similar bans in 2025; Global Trade Alert lists a ban "1 October 2025 to 31 January 2027" — [GTA](https://globaltradealert.org/state-act/94736-russia-temporary-ban-on-exports-of-diesel-fuel-marine-fuel-and-other-gas-oils-1-october-31-december-2025/)). Unresolved: UNVERIFIED which regime applied to producers on Oct 7. An Interfax item dated Sept 30, 2026 ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/119335/)) appeared in results but content was not read; it may be the latest word.
- Ukrainian strikes: "In the first eight months of 2026, a Russian refinery was successfully hit on average every three days"; Ukraine hit at least 24 of Russia's 34 large refineries in roughly 50 attacks over the past 100 days — [OilPrice/Webull, "As Ukraine Cripples Russian Refining, Global Diesel Markets Pay the Price"](https://www.webull.com/news/15236666683761664) (snippet; publication date unclear). IEA commentary the draft cites: [Russian refining sector struggles amid intensifying Ukrainian attacks](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/russian-refining-sector-struggles-amid-intensifying-ukrainian-attacks) (not read).
- IEA July OMR: Russian throughputs curtailed by attacks; IEA Sept OMR: the y/y decline in runs is concentrated in the Middle East, Russia and Asian crude importers — [IEA OMR July 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-July-2026); [Hellenic Shipping News on Sept OMR](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1147760) (snippets).
- Kuwait Times headline "Trump's Ukraine energy truce won't fix diesel crunch" suggests an energy-infrastructure truce was discussed in the period — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/49817/business/trumps-ukraine-energy-truce-wont-fix-diesel-crunch/) (snippet; date and substance not verified).

### Inferences
- Because the exact legal scope matters, the report should say "Russia banned gasoline exports for all exporters and diesel exports by non-producers through Jan 31, 2027" and flag that producers have had some access since Sept 1 (if still in force).

### Gaps
- Current (Oct 7) status of producer-level diesel and jet bans; reconciliation of the Sept 30 / Oct 31 extension items. Russian diesel export volumes for Aug/Sept not found.

## 5. China

### Takeaway
