## 1. Fed decision of Sept 16, 2026 (hike? range? vote? dots? statement? further hikes? next FOMC and pricing)

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: on Sept 16, 2026 the FOMC RAISED (hiked) the target range by 25 bp, from 3.50%-3.75% to 3.75%-4.00%, by a unanimous 12-0 vote, the first hike since July 2023. The draft's "đưa lãi suất lên vùng 3.75% đến 4%" is correct (not a cut). The draft's Part 4 claim that the Fed "signalled it could raise again this year" is also CONFIRMED (16 of 18 dot-plot participants see at least one more hike; median 2026 dot 4.1%), but as of early October markets had largely dropped an OCTOBER hike (weak September jobs, dovish-leaning speeches) and still lean toward DECEMBER.

### Cited Findings

Announced Wed Sept 16, 2026, 2:00 pm ET with SEP/dot plot. ## 3. ECB: did it raise in 2026 because of energy?

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: the ECB hiked twice in 2026, both explicitly tied to the energy-price shock from the Iran war: June (deposit rate 2.00% to 2.25%) and Sept 10 (2.25% to 2.50%). A further hike on Oct 29 was ~60% priced as of Sept 24. The draft's "giá năng lượng buộc ECB tăng lãi suất" is accurate.

### Cited Findings
- **June 2026:** ECB raised the deposit facility rate from 2.00% to 2.25% (MRO 2.40%, marginal lending 2.65%), effective June 17; unanimous; first major central bank to hike on the back of war-driven energy prices. Source states announcement June 12 (the ECB's actual meeting day could be June 11; I could not check the ECB calendar - UNVERIFIED which). ECB said the situation "no longer qualified as a case for looking through the shock" — [itiger/Bloomberg](https://www.itiger.com/news/2650924226), [150sec](https://150sec.com/european-central-bank-raises-interest-rates-for-the-first-time-since-2023/), [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/ecb-raises-interest-rates-bolstering-bets-for-further-moves), [mtsinsights June summary](https://www.mtsinsights.com/summaries/9377/).
- **Sept 10, 2026:** DFR 2.25% to 2.50% (MRO to 2.65%, MLF to 2.90%), effective Sept 16; second hike of the year, held in Berlin; driven by accelerating inflation and new energy spike. Euro-area inflation Aug 2026: flash 3.3%, final 3.2% y/y; ECB projects ~3.0% average for 2026. Lagarde: Council "did not discuss future rate changes", "data dependent, meeting by meeting" — [newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/european-deposit-facility-rate-250-vs-exp-25-prev-225), [La Française](https://www.la-francaise.com/en-dk/investor/ecb-another-hike-with-no-commitment-on-what-follows.html), [Ahram/AP](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/576411.aspx), [kurs.com.ua](https://kurs.com.ua/en/novost/1478711-ecb-vdruge-za-rik-pidvishiv-stavku-cherez-priskorennja-infljaciyi).
- **Next:** Oct 28-29 meeting, decision Oct 29 at 14:15 CET; futures on Sept 24 implied ~60% odds of a hike to 2.75%; Sept flash inflation due Oct 2 (value not retrieved) — [Admiral Markets ECB October](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/ecb-meeting-october-2026).

### Inferences
- The ECB hiked into an energy supply shock even though it is "supply-driven" — consistent with the draft's point that central banks are tightening against fuel-driven inflation; but the ECB's stated reason is risk of second-round effects / expectations de-anchoring (inflation 3.3% vs 2% target), not credit.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve ECB press releases themselves (blocked); June meeting date (11 vs 12) and the Oct 2 flash HICP figure not verified; Dec 2025-May 2026 ECB path assumed unchanged at 2.00% (consistent with "from 2%").

## 7. Do diesel/energy costs link to these credit events? (draft: they predate the diesel shock)

### Takeaway
The draft's chronology claim is largely CONFIRMED for Renovo (Nov 2025, ~4 months before the war), TCPC's NAV cut (Jan 2026) and Loparex's roots (2024 distressed exchange; Q1-Q2 2026 marks, June interest miss), and no source links any of these specific credit events to diesel. But the broader ENERGY shock (war from Feb 28, oil +72-94% in March) overlapped Q1-Q2 2026 stress, so "predates the diesel shock" is accurate while "predates the energy shock" would be only partly true for Loparex.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline: Renovo Chapter 7 Nov 3, 2025; TCPC NAV announcement Jan 23, 2026; Loparex $1.5bn sounded out Jan 28, 2026; Q1 redemption wave (Blackstone ~$3.7bn in Jan-Feb); war began Feb 28; Brent roughly +72% to ~$103 by Apr 1, as high as $118 (peak Mar) — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/middle-market-debt-weekly-iran-war-triggers-historic-oil-surge-while-private-credit-contagion-spreads-to-blackrock/); Loparex marks 88 (Dec 31) / 63 (Mar 31) / 5 (Jun 30); June interest missed; ECB hike June; diesel retail record Sept 8; crack spread >$100 on Aug 17 (draft); Moody's Aug 6; Fed hike Sept 16.
- Argument for a link (opinion/trade-press level only): ABF Journal's "Middle Market Debt Weekly" says the energy shock creates cascading risks for middle-market lenders (transportation/logistics margin compression from fuel, rising manufacturing inputs, consumer demand weakness) in a "stagflation" setup; Counterpunch ("The Iran War is Killing Private Credit", Mar 13) is an opinion piece, low weight — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/middle-market-debt-weekly-war-redemptions-rate-uncertainty-collide-as-middle-market-lenders-navigate-a-triple-stress-test/), [Counterpunch](https://www.counterpunch.org/2026/03/13/the-iran-war-is-killing-private-credit/).
- Trucking: freight "recession" since 2022 with record cost per mile ($2.336 in 2025) and owner-operator rates near 2022 levels; retail diesel $5.64 (Aug) — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/bmo-trucking-credit-the-end-of-transparent-data-freightwaves), [Summar freight update Aug 2026](https://summar.com/freight-market-update-august-2026/). This shows transportation borrowers were pressured, but I found no named private-credit loan loss tied to diesel.
- Loparex's stated trigger was a failed equity M&A deal; sources do not attribute it to energy or diesel.

### Inferences (strongest counter-arguments to the draft's causal story)
1. Idiosyncratic/pre-existing: Renovo (PIK, roll-up, consumer remodeling hit by high rates and weak housing turnover), Loparex (2024 distressed exchange, 2027 maturities) were fragile on their own.
2. Rates, not diesel, are the likelier macro channel: 10-year at 5-5.3% and mortgage rates 7%+ are plausibly what hit remodeling/housing-linked borrowers and stalled refis (the draft itself leans on this).
3. Redemption/valuation-confidence dynamics (non-traded BDC runs, Blue Owl 45% share drop) were a sector liquidity story that began in Q1.
4. Timing: diesel peaked Aug-Sept, after Loparex's marks and Moody's default, so diesel cannot explain those marks; the earlier March oil spike (Brent ~$118-119) and broader war-driven rate expectations could have.
5. Fed's own description of the economy at the Sept hike (solid activity, resilient spending) is a counter-narrative to "diesel is already killing demand"; the +29k payrolls and 4.2% unemployment on Oct 2 are the best new support for the draft's demand-destruction thesis.

### Gaps
- No filing/credit-agency text saying diesel or fuel costs contributed to Renovo, Loparex, or any BDC non-accrual; no sector-level default breakdown by industry (transportation vs housing) found.
